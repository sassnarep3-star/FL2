Markets, singles, parlays and prop combos

Decision this page serves: which instruments we originate on, and where a combination is genuinely better than its parts. Prices, vig and hold columns are blank on purpose: no market source is licensed or captured (FL-014), so a number there would be invented.

Single bet vs parlay vs prop combo — the actual trade-off

Single bet

  • One driver, one stake, cleanest measurement of a signal
  • Lowest vig burden per unit of edge
  • Best CLV signal: one leg ⇒ one close to beat
  • Most exposed to limits, since the book sees the same bet coming from everyone

Use for: every promotion decision. Nothing reaches the leaderboard through a parlay result.

Parlay / SGP

  • Turns two modest edges into a larger price, at a worse vig rate per leg
  • The only instrument where correlation is tradeable: the book’s multiplier implies a ρ, and we can compare it with ours
  • Push handling differs per book (reduces legs rather than voiding)
  • Correlated multi-game legs are voided by house rules outside SGP-type products — a legal constraint, not a modelling choice

Use for: stake efficiency and where the correlation mispricing is bigger than the vig penalty. Never to disguise one bet as five.

Prop combos

  • Highest model dependence: needs per-play rates and projected plays, both of which we lack (R-27, Q-01)
  • Availability flows into them most directly (a vacated target share, a short-handed secondary)
  • Thin limits, fast close, and the same-player stat pairs are often blocked as correlated
  • Grading is by official stats, so the data source is a dependency, not a detail

Use for: phase 3. The signals exist on this site (SIG-16/17/18) and are blocked for a reason worth respecting.

Instrument register

Pricing mechanics (implemented in engine/)

  • De-vig. Multiplicative normalisation across a market’s outcomes; the excess of summed implied probabilities over 1 is the vig. On a symmetric −110 / −110 market that is 4.545%, leaving a fair 0.500 each way.
  • Break-even. At −110 a bet must win 52.381% of decisions to break even; our hurdle for a signal is that rate after removing vig, not before.
  • Edge. Always measured against a de-vigged close, never against our own number or a soft open (Q-11, R-20).
  • Sizing. Fractional Kelly at ≤0.25 of full Kelly, computed jointly over correlated legs rather than leg by leg. Example: p=0.55 at −110 ⇒ full Kelly 5.5% ⇒ our stake cap 1.375%.
  • Parlays. Joint probability from a Gaussian copula; a book’s multiplier is inverted into an implied ρ, and the ticket is only taken when that ρ disagrees with ours by more than its own uncertainty.
  • Pushes. Modeled per book from its rule text. A push that reduces legs is not a refund, and treating it as one biases every alt-line parlay upward.

Both tables are computed arithmetic on stated marginal probabilities — they describe how the instruments behave, not what any book currently offers.

Consequence for the strategy layer: gate G-12 is a *legality* filter, not a quality filter. A quote that violates the book’s own rules is not a bad price, it is no price — which is exactly the class of error a leaderboard would otherwise reward.

Compliance and posture

  • This project originates numbers. It does not place bets, does not accept stakes, and does not publish tips or picks. Automated wager execution is out of scope until operator terms and regulator permission are read (Q-15).
  • Where the owner is located (Mexico), the operating regime is a permit regime administered by SEGOB’s games and drawings directorate as described by a market tracker — every element of that description (permit model, the 50% IEPS rate said to apply from 1 January 2026, restriction to each sport’s top professional league, operator counts) reached us through an aggregator and is not verified here: FL-011. The primary gazette text is a required next step, not an optional one.
  • Data licensing is unresolved in both directions: what we may read from league and club pages (FL-001…Q-01), and what a licensed sports-data vendor permits for derived models (S-19).
  • Reward design deliberately pays capital allocation rather than cash (SG-23) so the competition cannot become the product.
  • Harm note: every figure on this site is a hypothesis under test. Variance dominates small samples — the arithmetic on the methodology page shows how many bets a hit rate needs before it counts as evidence at all, and at a 53% rate that number is far larger than anyone is willing to wait for. If anything built here is ever staked, set limits before starting, treat lost stakes as unrecoverable, and use the support services published by the relevant regulator.
