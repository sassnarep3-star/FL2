The one-paragraph version. The plumbing the brief asked for exists and is largely sound: 148 verified finals, a dated price store, frozen pre-registered Week 11 forecasts, a leaderboard that separates signal from staking, and a designation calibration measured with intervals instead of folklore. What is missing is not code but three decisions and one discipline: the price basis that every CLV gate waits on, the snap-count or inactive evidence that would let a backup replacing a starter be confirmed rather than suspected, and the habit of treating the league's own calendar (report weeks, final scores) as a constraint instead of an inconvenience. Today's own checks also found a false block: the injury archive that three requirements say does not exist does exist, on nfl.com.

Start here

How this list relates to the board. The board ranks what it can compute: register width, which not-done readiness rows a step touches, and how many other suggestions wait on it. That arithmetic puts fl2-lines-SG-01 (turn the snapshot workflow on) first. The list below is the same information read as a decision, so it leads with the two rows nothing in the repository can close on its own - DS-06 (the price basis, an owner call) and hub-SG-07 (what a "reward" even is). Where the two disagree, the board's arithmetic is the record and this list is the argument.
  1. Decide the price policy and write the decision down (board row DS-06; fl2-lines-SG-04 asks the same of the fl2-lines register). No 2026 row in either price store has both an opening and a closing number (data/price_snapshots.csv), so closing-line-value gates are unsatisfiable until an outside benchmark is chosen. Buying an archive, recording our own dated snapshots, or dropping CLV are all defensible; leaving the question open is not, because it silently blocks everything downstream.
  2. Re-derive the designation calibration from the primary source (DS-01). The published rates are already good; the premise that they cannot be verified against the league's archive is wrong (the two archived pages that render today). This also opens the 2025 and 2026 seasons for the "is the label a feature" test.
  3. Stop the site contradicting itself (DS-02 for the standings, DS-04 for the grading refusal). The published standings table disagrees with the official page for 24 of 31 sampled clubs while the feed behind it is correct, and one Week 10 game was live at 148 finals. Both are build-time derivations, not research.
  4. Give reviewers one queue (DS-03). Five registers of suggestions with two id formats became one board with 68 decisions, deduped, with every original id kept.
  5. Do not build depth from what the club charts already disprove (hub-SG-01 and fl2-lines-SG-02 , at 3rd and 12th on the board). 2026 snap counts held by this repository: 0. Until an inactive list or snap share exists, a replacement is suspected, never named - which is what the site says today, and it should keep saying it.
