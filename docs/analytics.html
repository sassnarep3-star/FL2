Model pathology diagnosis

The fitted model scored 42.9% straight-up while the naive 'better point differential' rule scored 60.3% on the same window. Why?

With a free intercept the fit becomes home-leaning in a season where home teams win only 48.91% of games. The free-intercept model picks the home side in 82 of 105 graded games (78.1%) and wins just 43.9% of those picks; its overall straight-up accuracy (42.86%) lands below the 48.91% home base rate it is implicitly betting on. Forcing the intercept to zero makes the forecast change sign with the differential: the model then splits 50 home / 55 away, wins 54.0% of its home picks and 58.2% of its away picks, and straight-up accuracy rises to 56.19% with RMSE improving slightly (12.666 vs 12.763). Both variants still sit essentially on the no-skill benchmark for probability accuracy (Brier skill score -0.0098 and +0.0017 against a 0.2494 null).

Not a mystery of the sport - a specification artefact plus a genuine 2026 anomaly. Two lessons, both actionable: (1) a free intercept lets the model inherit a home-team bias that does not exist in 2026 data, so any future model must be audited for side-imbalance before its accuracy is believed; (2) even after fixing it, the Brier skill score is +0.0017, i.e. no demonstrable skill. Do not bet this.
