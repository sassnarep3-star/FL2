Availability & depth

The core goal is to know who may play, who actually plays, and which role is displaced—not simply count names on an injury list. This repository currently contains no player-level records.

Verification boundary: the official NFL injury-report page currently displays player, position, listed injury, practice participation and game status. It does not, by itself, prove the full depth-chart order, expected snap share, substitution, or a defensive/offensive unit's performance effect. Those fields remain blank until separately evidenced.

Starter and backup replacement tracking (both sides of the ball)

For each game, create a versioned lineup map for offense and defense. A player’s observed role must be supported by a pre-game official club announcement or an explicit, reproducible participation rule. Historical usage can be computed from official game records only after play-level or snap-count sources, definitions and identity joins are confirmed. Do not infer “first-string” solely from a player’s position, injury listing, or one high snap count.

Update and audit protocol

  1. Snapshot official report at each publication change; preserve retrieval time (UTC), report week, source URL, page text or permitted archival reference, and revision hash.
  2. Keep status categories as published. Build a separate state timeline: report-listed → announced active/inactive (if verified) → observed participation → postgame official statistics.
  3. Store offense and defense separately, with role evidence and source links per player-game. Never silently forward-fill a missing report.
  4. Measure injury/absence effects using comparable role opportunities and out-of-sample tests; report intervals and sample sizes. Avoid causal language unless study design supports it.
  5. On stale source, conflicting source, uncertain identity, or undated depth chart, flag the record and abstain from using it in a live signal.
