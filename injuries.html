What this week’s data teaches the model

1 · The designation column is sparse on purpose

of captured rows have no game status at all. A naive pipeline treats those as “available”, or as a data error; both are wrong. The reporting rule requires a designation for players who did not practise fully, so a DNP-with-no-designation row is itself information: most such rows on a club page carry a non-injury rest tag (e.g. NIR - Rest for Darrisaw and H. Smith on the club report, blank injury field on the league report). Encode it as a distinct state, not as a null.

2 · Practice status and designation disagree in both directions

Examples in this snapshot: a defender listed Out after a limited Friday practice (Norman-Lott); a pass rusher listed Questionable while tagged as resting (Garrett); a lineman Out after a full practice (Green II); several Out after DNP. The model should therefore treat the pair (trajectory, designation) as the feature, not either one alone (SIG-07), and keep a per-club count of divergences as its own diagnostic (FL-009).

3 · Two official feeds, two vocabularies

The report writes S, T, LB, DE; the roster page writes SAF, OT, OLB, DB, DL. Joining on the position string loses rows silently and biases the depth model toward whichever spelling survived. A crosswalk table (FL-003) is a prerequisite for R-09, not a refinement.

4 · Concussion and illness rows behave differently from structural injuries

This snapshot contains several Concussion rows and a handful of Illness rows, including a player listed Out while his team’s depth chart implies no structural loss. Their availability distribution is not the same object as a sprain’s, and neither is a resting veteran’s. That is a modelling requirement (R-08), and a reason to store the injury text verbatim rather than a normalised code we invent.

5 · The Friday report is not the end of the story

Availability changes between the final designation and the inactive list (~90 minutes before kickoff). Until the inactive lists are captured and diffed (Q-02), every availability feature here is a prior, not a fact about who played — which is exactly why the engine keeps the model output as a distribution.

6 · Rest weeks are visible in the data before they are visible in the line

Two Jaguars veterans are listed Out with “Not injury related – resting player” on a 6-2 team’s report; a Texans linebacker carries “Groin, Not injury related - personal matter” in the same field. Composite injury strings — where a real injury and a non-injury reason share one cell — are a parsing case the schema must allow (a list of reasons, not a single value).
