Reading the table for signal, not for story

Two columns matter for a rating model and the rest are context. PF and PA are what a power rating consumes; PCT, division and conference records are what a narrative consumes. A model built on PCT is a model built on 9 games of results, which is the definition of small-sample noise (see Luck vs Skill).

Note the extremes, because they are the cases where a rating model and the win column disagree most — which is where any value in this project would come from:

  • Jaguars are +108 in net points at 6-2 — the strongest differential in the AFC. A rating model likes them more than the record does.
  • Chargers are −98 at 0-8, and Eagles are −73 at 2-7. Their records are worse than their differentials, which is the mirror case.
  • Panthers are +24 in net points at 3-5, and Buccaneers +24 at 3-6. Positive differentials with losing records are the classic regression candidates — but see the warning below before treating that as a signal.
Regression to the mean is a hypothesis, not a finding. The observations above are descriptive of one snapshot. Turning "positive differential, losing record" into a bet requires multi-season evidence and an odds feed, which is Gate G3 and is currently unmet for this and every other strategy.
