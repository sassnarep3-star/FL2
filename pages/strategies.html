Strategy Origination

Twelve falsifiable signal hypotheses across single bets, parlays and prop combos — with an honest status for every one.

Twelve candidate signals across single bets, parlays and player-prop combinations. Each is written as a falsifiable hypothesis with a named data requirement and a stated failure mode — including the two that our own data has already killed.

Zero of 12 strategies currently pass all ten gates. That is the honest state, not a failure of the exercise. A strategy that passes all gates without odds data would be a red flag, because gates G3, G4, G5 and G9 are impossible to clear without historical prices.

How we would originate our own line

This is the part that does not need a proprietary feed. It needs a rating, a home-field term, and a price. The specification below is deliberately simple so that every term can be audited.

StepTermInputSource status
1Offensive ratingPoints per game, opponent-adjustedhave it — nfl.com standings + stats
2Defensive ratingPoints allowed per game, opponent-adjustedhave it — nfl.com standings
3Efficiency cross-checkNGS offensive and defensive EPA per playhave it — nfl.com/ngs/trends
4Availability adjustmentInjury status × depth-string weightpartial — injuries complete, depth 2/32
5Home-field termLeague-wide home advantage estimatemissing — requires multi-season results
6SpreadRating differential + home termmethod only — uncalibrated
7TotalExpected combined scoring from the same ratingsmethod only — uncalibrated
8PriceConvert our probability to odds, compare to closemissing — no odds feed

The Week 10 Slate page applies a naive version of steps 1–3 and shows the resulting margin for all 14 games, labelled as a rating differential and explicitly not a line for sale. A rating differential is not a spread: it has no home-field term and no market information.

Parlays and prop combos — why they are a different problem

A two-leg parlay priced at −110 each pays decimal 3.6446. The effective hold is 8.88%, not 4.76%. You are paying the tax twice. To be worth placing, the joint probability must exceed what the book charged you for — and that joint probability depends entirely on the correlation between the legs, which the book models and we currently cannot.

Every strategy in detail

ST01 · Rest-vs-urgency situational fade

Bet shapeSingle bet · Spread
Statussignal-research   gates passed: 0/10   blocked at: G3 (needs historical odds)
ThesisTeams eliminated from contention (playoff probability <5% per NGS) may rest starters late; the market prices reputation, not motivation.
Signal inputsNGS playoff probability (S06); official injury/rest designations (S01)
Data requiredNGS trends + injury report + closing spread history
Why it might workMotivation is a real, documented coaching behaviour; rest designations appear in the official report.
What would kill itIf playoff-probability-driven rest is already fully priced, edge is zero, and sample is tiny.

ST02 · Depth-string deficiency fade (defense)

Bet shapeSingle bet · Spread / team total
Statusblocked   gates passed: 1/10   blocked at: G3 + G8 (depth coverage 2/32 clubs)
ThesisWhen a defence loses a starter and the next man up is 2nd-or-3rd string at a premium position, opponent scoring rises more than the line implies.
Signal inputsOfficial injury report game status (S01) + official club depth chart (S11/S12)
Data requiredInjury report + per-club depth charts + closing lines
Why it might workDepth is a published fact; the join between 'Out' and 'who replaces him' is objective.
What would kill itDepth charts are club-published and 'unofficial' by their own labelling; they can lag reality.

ST03 · Depth-string deficiency fade (offense, O-line first)

Bet shapeSingle bet · Team total under
Statusblocked   gates passed: 1/10   blocked at: G3 + G8
ThesisO-line attrition is the highest-leverage offensive injury because it degrades every skill player at once.
Signal inputsS01 game status filtered to T/G/C + S11 depth strings
Data requiredInjury report + depth charts + team total history
Why it might workSacks-taken vs PPG correlation measured at r=-0.621 (R2 38.6%), so pressure does move scoring.
What would kill itPositional criticality is not officially published; any weighting is our assumption.

ST04 · Pass-efficiency momentum vs opponent pass defence

Bet shapeSingle bet · Spread
Statussignal-research   gates passed: 0/10   blocked at: G3

What would kill itExtremely well-known signal; expected to be priced.

ST05 · Turnover-regression fade

Bet shapeSingle bet · Spread / total
Statussignal-research   gates passed: 0/10   blocked at: G3 + G6 (needs multiple seasons to prove regression)
ThesisDefensive interceptions are volatile. Teams winning on turnovers should regress; fade the team whose record is turnover-driven.
Signal inputsS04 defense interceptions (published)
Data requiredInterception data + closing lines + multi-season history
Why it might workMeasured: def INT vs points allowed r=-0.561, i.e. turnovers do correlate with scoring in-season.
What would kill itRequires multi-season data to separate luck from skill; one snapshot cannot.

ST07 · Two-leg same-game stack (QB yards + WR1 yards)

Bet shapeParlay · Player props
Statusblocked   gates passed: 1/10   blocked at: G1 (no player data) + G7 (no event-level rho)
ThesisSame-game legs are positively correlated, so the true joint probability exceeds the naive product and a standard parlay price can be +EV.
Signal inputsPlayer prop lines (not ingested)
Data requiredPlayer-level game logs, both legs, same game
Why it might workComputed: at rho=0.40 a two-leg -110 parlay moves from -8.9% EV to +15.0% EV.
What would kill itSportsbooks price correlated same-game parlays with their own correlation model; the edge may be exactly zero after their adjustment.

ST08 · Two-leg negative-correlation fade (RB rushing + opposing QB)

Bet shapeParlay · Player props
Statusblocked   gates passed: 1/10   blocked at: G1 + G7
ThesisGame-script legs are negatively correlated: a heavy rushing game suppresses opponent passing volume.
Signal inputsPlayer prop lines (not ingested)
Data requiredPlayer-level game logs
Why it might workComputed: at rho=-0.40 a two-leg parlay is -32.8% EV versus -8.9% at rho=0. Standard pricing badly misprices these.
What would kill itIf the book already applies a negative-correlation haircut, the 'find' disappears.

ST09 · Injury-news latency (first-mover on official report)

Bet shapeSingle bet · Spread
Statusblocked   gates passed: 1/10   blocked at: G3 + G9 (no tick data)
ThesisThe official report drops on a fixed deadline; acting inside the window between publication and line adjustment may capture value.
Signal inputsS10 filing deadlines; S01 report content

ST11 · Rest-designation fade (JAX-style 'not injury related - resting')

Bet shapeSingle bet · Team total under
Statussignal-research   gates passed: 1/10   blocked at: G3 + G8
ThesisA starter ruled Out for rest is a real availability loss that the report flags explicitly.
Signal inputsS01 rows carrying 'Not injury related - resting player'
Data requiredInjury report + depth charts + team totals
Why it might workThe designation is official, so detection is objective (2 such rows observed in Week 10: Jessie Bates, Jordan Davis).
What would kill itRest is often announced early, so it is likely already in the price.

ST12 · Originated-line divergence (our number vs the close)

Bet shapeSingle bet · Spread + total
Statusproposed   gates passed: 0/10   blocked at: G3 + G5 (no odds archive)
ThesisBuild our own power ratings, originate a line, and bet only when our number differs from the close beyond a calibrated threshold.
Signal inputsAll of S02/S03/S04/S06 inputs
Data requiredTeam stats + closing lines + full season history
Why it might workThis is the cleanest, most auditable path: no proprietary data needed.
What would kill itWithout an odds archive we cannot calibrate the threshold, and the market is efficient enough (Q11) that the threshold may be unreachable.
