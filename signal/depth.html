Depth accuracy

Does the published depth chart predict who plays? Measured, not assumed.

The question this answers. The request asks to track second-, third- and fourth-string players replacing starters. That whole feature assumes the club's published depth chart knows who the next man up is. This page tests the assumption instead of relying on it.

The gap is only about 10.23 points. A chart that precisely described the starting lineup would separate first from second string far more sharply. The honest reading is that the published depth chart is a weak discriminator of who actually plays, so it should not be treated as a reliable replacement map without further evidence.

This measures appearance only — whether the player took at least one snap, including special teams. It does not measure whether he started, or whether he out-snapped the backup. The stronger version of the test is listed in the Questions register as still open.

Caveats

  • Presence in snap counts means at least one snap, including special teams.
  • Players traded or renamed mid-season may fail the name join.
  • Depth charts may be published at a different cadence than weekly, so some team-weeks may reuse a stale chart.
  • This does not establish that the depth chart is a reliable replacement map; it only quantifies how often the listed player appears at all.
