Correlation

Measured same-game leg correlation, because parlay pricing assumes there is none.

Why a number is needed here. Parlay pricing multiplies leg probabilities, which is only correct when the legs are independent. We measured the joint frequency of two legs landing in the same game and compared it with that product. The difference is the pricing error.

The honest reading

phi of 0.013 is close to zero. For the all-games bucket, treating these two legs as independent is defensible to within about a third of a percentage point. The correlation is concentrated in large spreads, where phi reaches 0.058. That means a parlay combining a big favourite to cover with the over is the case where the independence assumption actually costs money — and it is the case most people bet.

This is a historical base rate. It is not a forecast, not an edge, and not adjusted for the vig.

What is deliberately not published

Player-prop correlations. They can be computed from the outcomes we hold, but a combo cannot be priced without prop prices, which we do not have (Q02). Publishing a prop correlation as though it were a tradable edge would be exactly the kind of unverified claim this project forbids. The work is blocked, and the block is shown here rather than hidden.

Caveats

  • This is a historical base rate, not a forecast and not an edge.
  • Pushes are excluded from both legs rather than treated as losses.
  • A fair market should show a small positive correlation here; a large one would suggest a data error.
  • The vig is not applied; a parlay's true cost also depends on the book's correlation adjustment.
