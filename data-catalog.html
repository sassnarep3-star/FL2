Access notes that change the architecture

The league report is scrapable content, not an API

nfl.com/injuries/ serves its tables in the document itself, which is why a text-mode fetch produced a usable Week 10 table. Consequences: (1) a crawler can work without a key today; (2) it is bound by whatever the terms of use say about automated access — which is unverified (Q-01); (3) the content is paginated by the reader, so a capture is a partial artefact unless every chunk is taken (FL-002, FL-005).

There is no official odds source, by construction

Injury reports and roster status are league-published facts because clubs are obliged to file them. Prices are not: they belong to operators. So the “official” tier for anything market-related does not exist, and the only honest options are a licensed odds feed or our own timestamped captures (S-19, S-20) — which is why the CLV/hold/edge columns on this site are blank rather than “≈4.5%”.

Club pages carry what the league page collapses

The club report adds per-day practice participation and the non-injury-rest labels, and its prose can even disagree with its own table on an injury label (FL-004). The ingestion design keeps both and treats their disagreement as data (a divergence count), not as an error to be resolved on the fly.

Aggregators disagree, so they get a distinct tier

Two trackers gave different counts of legal US jurisdictions (38 vs 39), and the Mexican operating details exist only in aggregator prose right now. They are useful for pointing at primary documents and useless as inputs — hence a tier of their own, and the flags (FL-010, FL-011).

Sandbox constraint on “live” data

This environment can reach only a small set of package hosts, so no page here can re-fetch at load time, and the site is a snapshot by construction (FL-015). If freshness matters, the correct design is a scheduled job that regenerates the data files and commits them, so the published site always equals the repo.
