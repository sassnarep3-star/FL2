Line Origination & Signal Strategies

Research and design for generating useful NFL betting signals — single bets, parlays, and player-prop combinations — with validation gates, correlation controls, and luck removed from evaluation. Everything on this page is a proposed design by the FL2 project, pending review in Suggestions; where a proposal depends on a data source, the missing information is linked to the open questions in Questions & Answers. No line or odds values appear here because no verified odds feed has been selected yet — those cells stay blank rather than guessed.

1. Strategy lifecycle

Every strategy follows the same gate-driven path. A strategy that has not passed the gates cannot appear on the Leaderboard and cannot be acted on.

  1. Propose — define the signal family, bet type, and expected edge mechanism.
  2. Register — record the strategy specification (signals, filters, sizing rule) in the strategy register.
  3. Validate — run the validation gates in section 4 in order. Failure at any gate stops the strategy.
  4. Correlate — compute correlation values against existing strategies (section 5); combos are sized or rejected based on the result.
  5. Score — evaluate with the luck-removed skill metrics (section 6), out-of-sample only.
  6. Rank — post the validated score to the Leaderboard.

3. Bet-type construction

3.1 Single bets

  • One signal → one market (spread, total, moneyline). Selection rule: signal strength must exceed the minimum-edge threshold (gate G7) after the book's vig.
  • Confidence tiers map to staking per the approved bankroll rules (Q10).
  • Every single-bet strategy is scored on the luck-removed metrics in section 6, not on raw win rate.

3.2 Parlays

  • Legs are drawn from independent signal families where possible; legs from the same family (e.g., two injury-driven legs) are treated as correlated and sized down or rejected per the correlation threshold (section 5, Q5).
  • No parlay is constructed from legs whose correlation value exceeds the approved cap.
  • Parlay-specific gates: minimum leg count, leg-selection rule, and per-parlay bankroll cap.

3.3 Player-prop combinations

  • Player props are combined only across players and games with verified data (injury status, depth position, role weighting).
  • Same-game player props are treated as correlated (shared game environment); cross-game combinations are evaluated with the correlation matrix before acceptance.
  • Each prop leg requires its own edge check (gate G7); the combination is accepted only if the aggregate correlation profile passes the cap.

5. Correlation values

Methodology (proposed): for every pair of strategies and every pair of bet legs within a combo, compute correlation on rolling windows using both Pearson and Spearman coefficients, and act on the conservative one. Actual correlation values cannot be produced yet — they require play-level and market data that is not yet selected (Q3, Q5, S3, S5). Until then the correlation matrix is intentionally empty; no values are estimated or filled in.
