Luck vs Skill

The arithmetic of removing luck: how many bets it takes, how often luck wins a crown, and how much skill can be staked safely.

"Remove luck from the equation" is the hardest part of the brief and the easiest part to fake. This page is the arithmetic. None of it is a claim about a strategy; all of it is a constraint any strategy must obey.

The sample-size arithmetic

To distinguish a true win rate from the break-even rate at 80% power and 5% one-sided significance:

True win rateEdge over break-evenBets required
52.5%0.12 pp1,088,916
53.0%0.62 pp40,230
53.5%1.12 pp12,305
55.0%2.62 pp2,243
57.5%5.12 pp585
60.0%7.62 pp263
The practical consequence. A real, exploitable edge in a market this liquid is usually small — a couple of points. Detecting a 1.1-point edge needs 12,305 settled bets. A full NFL season offers roughly 270 games; a single-team strategy on one market yields a few dozen bets. You cannot prove a small edge inside a season. Any leaderboard that claims otherwise is measuring variance.

Staking: how much skill can be extracted without being ruined

Even a real edge can be destroyed by over-staking. Kelly gives the growth-optimal fraction; fractional Kelly gives most of the growth at a fraction of the drawdown.

True win ratePriceFull KellyHalf KellyQuarter Kelly
53.0%-1101.3%0.65%0.33%
55.0%-1105.5%2.75%1.38%
57.0%-1109.7%4.85%2.42%
55.0%+10010.0%5.0%2.5%
60.0%-150-0.0%-0.0%-0.0%

The last row is the useful one: a 60% win rate at −150 has negative expected value, so Kelly sizes it at zero. That is the whole argument for Gate G5 in one line — a high win rate at a bad price is a losing strategy, and the staking formula refuses to fund it.

How skill is actually demonstrated here

  1. Closing-line value above zero with a confidence interval that excludes zero. This is the cleanest available proof, because it does not require winning every bet — only consistently taking the better side of the price.
  2. Out-of-sample profit across at least three held-out seasons. In-sample profit is worthless.
  3. A positive skill residual after controlling for opponent strength. This is what separates "good team" from "good process".
  4. Reproducibility. If the bet list changes when the model is re-run on the same data, there is no model.

What this page does not claim

No strategy in this repository has been graded. There is no win rate, no ROI and no CLV figure anywhere in this project, because the odds data required to compute one has not been ingested. Every number above is a constraint on future results, not a report of past ones. The moment a "performance" number appears without a sourced price history behind it, it should be treated as fabricated.
