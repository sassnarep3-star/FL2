Validation Gates

Ten sequential gates with numeric thresholds, and the arithmetic that explains why most strategy leaderboards rank luck.

Ten gates, in the order they must be cleared. Each has a numeric threshold and a named failure mode. A strategy that fails any gate does not advance — it is not "promising but unproven".

Gates are sequential. Clearing G7 is meaningless if G3 failed, because G7 asks how much the edge is worth, not whether one exists. The order is the point.

The leaderboard, and why it usually lies

A leaderboard ranks strategies. If it ranks raw win rate or raw profit, it ranks luck. Three specific mechanisms guarantee this, and all three are arithmetic, not opinion.

Read this row: at 500 bets, anything below a 57.9% true rate is indistinguishable from noise. A leaderboard populated at 500 bets is ranking noise.

3 · Many strategies guarantee a false winner

25.14% chance that a single zero-edge strategy shows a 55%+ win rate over 200 bets. Across 500 such strategies, the probability that at least one looks outstanding is 100.0%. This is not a corner case; it is the expected outcome.

The scoring rule this implies

  1. Pre-register every strategy before it is tested. An unregistered strategy cannot be ranked. (Gate G10.)
  2. Rank on closing-line value first, profit second. CLV has far lower variance, so it separates skill from luck sooner. (Gate G4.)
  3. Apply a false-discovery correction across the whole registry, not per strategy. (Gate G10.)
  4. Show, do not rank, below 500 settled bets; rank only above 2,000. (Decision question Q04.)
  5. Report the skill residual, not the win rate. (Gate G6.)

What "reward" should mean

The brief asks for rewards for top-performing strategies but does not define reward. This matters more than it looks: a reward tied to wagering volume can turn a research project into a regulated gambling product. Recommended: reward internal ranking, priority for further research, and publication — not money and not staking. This is recorded as Q18 and needs your decision.

Every gate in detail

G1 · Data availability and provenance

DefinitionEvery input field resolves to an official source with a retrieval date, or the strategy is blocked.
TestWalk each field to its source_id; confirm the live page still contains the value.
Pass threshold100% of fields sourced; 0 blanks in required fields.
Why it existsA model fed by an unsourced field cannot be audited, and cannot be defended once it loses money.

G2 · Effect exists in the data

DefinitionThe hypothesised relationship is measurably present before any bet logic is written.
TestPearson r with a bootstrap 95% CI on the ingested sample.
Pass threshold|r| >= 0.20 AND the 95% CI excludes zero.
Why it existsWithout a measurable effect you are fitting noise. ST06 is the worked example of a fail.

G3 · Out-of-sample profitability vs the close

DefinitionThe strategy beats the closing line on data it was not fitted on.
TestWalk-forward test: fit on seasons 1..n, test on n+1. Grade against closing prices.
Pass thresholdPositive ROI with a 95% CI lower bound > 0 over >= 3 held-out seasons.
Why it existsIn-sample profit is the single most common way backtests lie.

G4 · Closing-line value (CLV)

DefinitionEven in a losing stretch, did the strategy systematically beat the close?
TestMean CLV in cents of implied probability, per bet.
Pass thresholdMean CLV > 0 with 95% CI excluding zero.
Why it existsCLV is a far lower-variance skill indicator than win rate; it separates skill from luck faster than ROI does.

G5 · Break-even hurdle cleared

DefinitionReported edge is stated net of vig, not gross.
TestRecompute EV per bet using the actual price paid, per bet, not an average.
Pass thresholdNet EV > 0 at the price actually available.
Why it existsAt -110 you need 52.38% just to break even; a 55% 'winner' at -150 is a loser.

G6 · Luck decomposition

DefinitionSplit results into skill and variance components and show the skill component is positive.
TestCompare realised wins to a Luck-Expected Wins model; report the skill residual and its CI.
Pass thresholdSkill residual > 0 with 95% CI excluding zero, after controlling for opponent strength.
Why it existsOne-score game records and turnover margins are dominated by variance.

G7 · Correlation honestly modelled

DefinitionFor multi-leg bets, the dependence between legs is estimated, not assumed to be zero.
TestEstimate rho from event-level data; reprice the bet with a copula; compare to the offered price.
Pass thresholdThe repriced EV stays positive after the book's own correlation adjustment.
Why it existsComputed: rho=0.40 flips a two-leg parlay from -8.9% to +15.0% EV. Assuming independence is a 24-point error.

G8 · Process repeatability (no manual intervention)

DefinitionThe signal fires from a scheduled job with no human judgement per bet.
TestRe-run the pipeline from a clean checkout and reproduce the exact bet list.
Pass thresholdByte-identical output on re-run; no manual overrides recorded.
Why it existsManual override is undetectable hindsight, and it is exactly what the owner asked to avoid.

G9 · Capacity and price availability

DefinitionThe edge survives realistic stake sizes and actual available limits.
TestCompare required stake to typical limits; simulate slippage.
Pass thresholdEdge remains positive at the target stake, after slippage.
Why it existsA 3% edge on a $50 limit is not a business.

G10 · Multiple-comparisons correction

DefinitionThe strategy's apparent edge survives a false-discovery correction across all tested strategies.
TestBenjamini-Hochberg FDR across the full registry, or a deflated Sharpe ratio.
Pass thresholdSurvives FDR at q = 0.10 across the registry.
Why it existsComputed: across 500 zero-edge strategies, at least one showing >=55% by luck is effectively certain. Without this gate, the leaderboard rewards noise.
