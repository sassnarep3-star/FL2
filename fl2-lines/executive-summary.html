What the numbers actually say

  • Home-field advantage in the 2015–2025 window is 1.74 points (n=2895), and the residual scale used for every probability is 14.201 points.
  • 2026 is a road-team season: home covers 40.88% of 137 graded games. A gate sized to one season would have aborted a correct sign convention; the gate now runs on 3,031 graded games (47.25% home cover) and the season figure is published as a finding rather than a gate.
  • Designations are not levels: Out plays 0.02% of the time (n=8736), Doubtful 0.92% (n=1522), Questionable 64.46% (n=13131). The model therefore consumes the surprise against these rates, never the label.
  • The margin calibration slope is 0.7586: the raw attack/defence decomposition is too sharp, and publishing it unshrunk would overstate the model.
  • Same-game parlay legs on this corpus look negatively joint (phi -0.1687, n=130) while the 2,927-game baseline is phi 0.0134. The 130-game interval contains the independence value, so nothing is multiplied together to rank a parlay.

Decisions requested from the owner

  1. Price source (QN-01/QN-02, SG-04): record our own dated consensus snapshots, buy a feed, or drop CLV from the gates. Every pricing gate depends on this and no choice is safe by default.
  2. Depth definition (QN-03/QN-04, SG-02): accept playing time as the definition of a string, or keep the backup-deficiency feature closed. Listed rank cannot be it: the clubs' own charts put multiple players at rank 1.
  3. Raw-data exposure (SG-08/QN-13): mirrors currently sit in a public Pages tree with unread terms.
  4. Six sections, one hub (SG-11/QN-14): adopt one navigation and one readiness view, or accept that readers will meet the same fixture at four tiers.
